Classically, confidence intervals are required to have consistent coverage across all values of the parameter. However, this will inevitably break down if the underlying estimation procedure is intentionally biased. For this reason, many efforts have focused on debiased versions of the lasso for interval construction. In the process of debiasing, however, the connection to the original estimates are obscured. In this work, we offer a different perspective focused on average coverage in contrast to individual coverage. This perspective results in intervals that better reflect the original assumptions, as opposed to debiased intervals, which often do not even contain the original lasso estimates. To this end we propose a method that gives approximately correct average coverage and compare this to debiased methods which attempt to produce correct individual coverage.
